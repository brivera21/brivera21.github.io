The Parts of a Neuron

A neuron collects signals at one end, decides whether they add up to enough, and sends the answer out the other end. Click any part of the cell, or any name in the list, to see what that part does. The parts are laid out left to right in the order a signal passes through them, and the four zones along the bottom are the same in every neuron. Terms and definitions follow Chapter 1 of the course textbook.

Click a structure in the diagram, or use the list below it. Shape and length are drawn for clarity, not to scale. On a phone, drag the diagram sideways to follow the axon.

Inside a terminal: the synapse up close

The last few hundred nanometers, magnified. Nothing here is drawn at the same scale as the neuron above.

The action potential arrives, voltage-gated calcium channels open, and calcium entering the terminal makes vesicles fuse with the membrane and empty their transmitter into the cleft. Transmitter crosses in well under a millisecond and binds receptors on the other side, opening channels there. That starts a fresh graded voltage change in the next neuron, and the whole sequence begins again.

How big is it really

Cell body (typical cortical neuron)10 to 25 µm acrosssmaller than the width of a hair, though a few giant cells run several times larger
Axon diameter0.2 to 20 µmthicker axons conduct faster
Axon lengthunder 1 mm to about 1 ma motor neuron reaching the foot spans the longer end
One myelin segmentroughly 0.1 to 1.8 mmlonger segments go with thicker axons
Node of Ranvierabout 1 µmthe bare gap between two segments
Synaptic cleft20 to 40 nmabout a thousandth the width of the cell body
Conduction speed0.5 to 120 m/sslowest unmyelinated to fastest myelinated
