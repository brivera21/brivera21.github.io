Animated pyramidal neuron with two recording electrodes: a dendritic pipette on the apical trunk whose trace marks each arriving graded potential as a positive or negative deflection, and a somatic pipette feeding a voltage meter and full-scale trace showing summation to threshold and an action potential.

What is each moving ball? Each ball is a wave of voltage change spreading along the membrane, not a packet of ions travelling down the cell. Ions only cross the membrane locally, and the local current they make carries the voltage change forward. Green means depolarising, pushing the cell toward firing (go): the excitatory graded potentials and the action potential. Red means hyperpolarising, pushing away from firing (no-go): the inhibitory graded potentials.

Ions are purposefully left out here for simplicity. The Na⁺ and K⁺ movements that create these voltage waves are covered in a separate zoomed-in diagram.

Time is not to scale. In real time an EPSP is far slower than a spike: The animation stretches the spike so you can see it; at true scale it would be a thin flash.

Two ways inputs summate

The soma adds inputs across both space and time. These are two separately named mechanisms.

Spatial summation

Different synapses on different dendrites fire at the same time. Their EPSPs meet at the soma and add.

Temporal summation

One synapse fires rapidly in succession. Each EPSP arrives before the last has decayed, so they pile up higher.

What to notice about action potentials

  1. 1Graded inputs vary. On electrode 1 the incoming signals differ in size and can be excitatory (up) or inhibitory (down). Their amplitude depends on the input.
  2. 2They shrink and add. By the soma (electrode 2) each signal is smaller, and the soma sums them all together over space and time (spatial and temporal summation, shown below).
  3. 3Threshold is a gate. The spike fires only if the summed potential lifts the axon hillock to about −55 mV. Below that, nothing happens.
  4. 4All-or-none. Once triggered the spike is always the same size (about +30 mV), no matter how large the input was. A bigger stimulus does not make a bigger spike.
  5. 5No decrement. Unlike the graded potentials, the action potential regenerates at each node and travels the whole axon without shrinking.
  6. 6Output. Reaching the terminals, the spike releases neurotransmitter, which becomes the graded input to the next cell.
