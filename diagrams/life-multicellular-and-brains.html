Life, Multicellular Organisms, and Brains

This diagram is about how a brain sits nested inside much larger patterns. Every ring lives inside the one around it, so a brain is contained within a nervous system, within a multicellular body, within life, within ordinary matter. Moving from the outer ring to the inner one means crossing four hard barriers: matter has to become alive, single cells have to become multicellular bodies, those bodies have to grow a nervous system, and that nervous system has to concentrate into a brain. Each red ring marks one crossing. A brain is not magical or sprung from the void, and it never leaves those bigger patterns behind. It stays nested within them, built from the same living cells, the crown of a stack of already remarkable machinery. Click a layer for its detail, or a red marker to jump to that threshold.

The five layers

The four thresholds, where the real barriers are

These are the crossings the diagram is built around. Each one is a genuine barrier, and for billions of years matter sat on the far side of it. The brain is the fourth crossing, and it rests on the earlier three, in order, because each is only possible on top of the one before. You cannot have a brain without a nervous system, a nervous system without a multicellular body, or a multicellular body without living cells. Each layer beneath the brain is already extraordinary in its own right, which is the whole point. The brain is the crown of a stack of amazing machinery, not a break from it.
