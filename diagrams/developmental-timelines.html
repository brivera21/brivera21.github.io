Developmental Timelines

Milestones from birth to puberty, plotted as ranges from early to late. Bars are published windows of normal variation; diamonds are the CDC's checklist ages, which since 2022 mark the age by which 75% or more of children do the thing, a late-typical marker rather than an average.

Hover a milestone

Note. The age axis is compressed past age five so infancy stays readable: the first 60 months take up most of the width, and the school years and puberty share the rest. Bars for the gross motor milestones are the WHO windows of achievement, the 1st to 99th percentile among healthy children; language bars are clinical ranges from the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia; reading and puberty bars are not CDC data at all and are drawn from Reading Rockets and from NICHD/AAFP ranges respectively. A diamond sitting at or past the right edge of its bar is not an error: the CDC deliberately placed its 2022 checklist ages where three quarters of children have already passed the milestone, so its ages land late in each window by design (Zubler et al., 2022).

Caveats.
  • Crawling has no diamond because the CDC removed it from the 2022 checklists: some healthy children never crawl, going from scooting to standing. Its bar is the WHO window for those who do.
  • A few CDC items name slightly different behaviors than the bars they sit on (pulling to stand versus standing with assistance; walking well versus first unaided steps), which is why two diamonds fall just outside their WHO windows.
  • Every range describes populations, not children. A single child outside a window is a reason to ask a pediatrician, not a diagnosis.
  • Puberty ranges use the standard clinical bounds: onset before 8 (girls) or 9 (boys) is evaluated as precocious, and absence by 13 or 14 as delayed (AAFP, 2017; NICHD).
