Ion Channels

Gated ion channels all do the same job. They let specific ions cross the membrane. What differs is the signal that opens them, and there are three kinds. Each panel has its own button. Add voltage opens the voltage-gated channels, Bind ligand opens the ligand-gated one, and Apply force opens the mechanically-gated one. Each opens with its own ions crossing. Ions are colored by species: Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺.

Press a channel's button to open it. Nothing opens on its own.

1🔓 Ungated (leak)

always open, no trigger needed

OPEN
This channel is a pore, not a valve: it is selective for K⁺ but has no directional preference. Any K⁺ that wanders into the pore from either side goes through. So K⁺ crosses both ways the whole time, and what changes is the balance. Near 0 mV the concentration gradient is unopposed, so outward crossings far outnumber inward ones (roughly 10 : 1). As the inside grows negative, its charge pulls more K⁺ back in, so the return traffic climbs to about 3 : 1 near −45 mV, and reaches 1 : 1 at the K⁺ equilibrium potential (≈−90 mV). There the meter sits still, but the traffic has not stopped. K⁺ is still pouring across both ways, now in balance, so there is no net change. (The cell's actual resting voltage is a little more positive, near −70 mV, because of a smaller Na⁺ leak; the Na⁺/K⁺ pump keeps the gradients topped up.) Compare it with the gated channels below, which stay shut until their own signal arrives.
2⚡ Voltage-gated

opened by a change in membrane voltage

CLOSED
The signal is membrane voltage. Each channel carries charged S4 "voltage-sensor" helices. When the membrane depolarizes, they slide outward and pull the gate open, letting Na⁺, K⁺, and Ca²⁺ through. Press Add voltage and a depolarization wave sweeps across the membrane, opening each channel as it passes and closing it again behind. The Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ channels close with a hinged gate whose two flaps meet to seal the pore; the K⁺ channel shows the other common style, a ball-and-chain "tail" that swings up and plugs the pore. These are the channels that make the action potential.
3💊 Ligand-gated

opened by a chemical binding to it

CLOSED
The signal is a chemical messenger. Two acetylcholine molecules drift in and dock in pockets between the subunits of this five-part channel. Their binding twists the subunits and tilts the pore-lining helices apart. It passes cations: Na⁺, K⁺, and some Ca²⁺.
4⇕ Mechanically-gated

opened by physical force on the membrane

CLOSED
The signal is force on the membrane. Curved blades bow the membrane into a dome. When something presses or pulls on the membrane (touch, pressure, or blood flow), its tension rises. That tension pulls the dome flat, and flattening opens the central pore. Piezo1 works this way, letting in Ca²⁺ and Na⁺. Its close relative Piezo2 is the main sensor for touch and proprioception.
5🔋 Na⁺/K⁺ pump

active transport that moves ions against their gradients

IDLE
This is not a gated channel. It is a pump. Instead of letting ions slide down their gradients, it spends energy to push them up theirs, and that is what builds the gradients the channels above rely on. Each turn of the cycle it carries 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in, powered by one ATP. It works by alternating access: 3 Na⁺ bind from the inside, ATP phosphorylates the pump and it flips to face outward and releases them, then 2 K⁺ bind from outside, a phosphate leaves, and it flips back to release the K⁺ inside. Because it exports 3 positive charges for every 2 it imports, it is electrogenic: each cycle moves a net +1 charge out, which itself makes the inside slightly more negative. Press Run pump to watch one cycle repeat.
