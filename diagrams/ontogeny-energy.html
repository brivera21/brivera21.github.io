Energy use across ontogeny

Whole-body basal metabolic rate from conception to the average age of death. The age axis is broken so that three short, high-interest windows — gestation, puberty, and a hypothetical pregnancy — get room to breathe, while the long uneventful stretches are compressed.

How to read it

Six panels, left to right, separated by // break marks on the axis. Three are magnified (shaded); three are compressed bridges. Because each panel has its own scale, horizontal distance means nothing across a break — only the vertical axis is continuous.

What the curves do

Conception → birthMetabolic rate climbs from zero to about 8 W. Nine months, given a whole panel — at true scale it is a sliver.
Infancy → childhoodRate rises steadily with body mass. Male and female are near-identical here; the small gap shown is body composition, not a real functional difference.
PubertyThe divergence. Male rate keeps climbing toward its peak; female rate levels off around 15 and plateaus. Dots mark onset (♀ ~11, ♂ ~12.5).
AdulthoodMale peaks near 95 W (~1,960 kcal/day) around 30; female plateaus near 70 W (~1,450 kcal/day) across 30–50.
PregnancyDashed line: basal rate rises roughly 20% by term, then returns. Absolute, not size-adjusted — see caveats.
Later lifeDecline from ~60 as both mass and cellular rate fall. Each curve terminates at zero at the mean age of death.

Caveats worth stating in class

  • These are constructed reference curves, not a measured cohort. Watts = body mass × mass-specific BMR, both from published reference values. The shapes are canonical; the individual points are not data.
  • The drop to zero is a graphical device. Metabolism does not decline along that trajectory to zero. The vertical is simply where the population mean age at death falls.
  • 76.5 and 81.4 are period life expectancy at birth (US, CDC 2024) — a synthetic-cohort statistic, not a forecast for anyone currently alive.
  • The pregnancy bump is absolute, not size-adjusted. Pontzer et al. (2021) found that expenditure adjusted for fat-free mass does not rise in pregnancy at all — it stays on the normal adult trend. The rise drawn here comes from the added maternal and fetal tissue. Plot this same figure per kilogram and the bump nearly vanishes.
  • Basal rate only. Total daily energy expenditure is roughly 1.4–2× these values once activity, thermoregulation and growth are included, and the pregnancy increment is larger on that measure.
  • Pre-pubertal sex separation is within noise. Do not let the two-colour treatment imply a meaningful childhood difference.
Sources: Pontzer et al., Daily energy expenditure through the human life course, Science 2021 (10.1126/science.abe5017); CDC NCHS, Mortality in the United States, 2024. Conversion used throughout: 1 kcal/day = 0.04843 W.
