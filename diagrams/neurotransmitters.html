Types of Neurotransmitters

Dozens of chemicals carry messages across synapses. They are grouped by what the molecule is made of, and the ones with the widest reach are grouped again by where their axons go.

Receptor subtypes

What a transmitter does at a synapse depends on the receptor waiting for it, and every transmitter has several. Ionotropic receptors hold a channel and act in a millisecond. Metabotropic receptors work through second messengers and act more slowly.

TransmitterKnown subtypesWhat they do
Glutamate AMPA, kainate, NMDA (ionotropic); mGluRs (metabotropic) AMPA receptors are the most plentiful receptors in the brain. NMDA receptors are central to memory formation.
GABA GABAA (ionotropic); GABAB (metabotropic) GABAA lets Cl− into the cell and hyperpolarizes it fast. Benzodiazepines act here, which is why they calm the whole system down.
Acetylcholine Nicotinic (ionotropic); muscarinic (metabotropic) Nicotinic receptors drive muscle contraction. Both types carry cholinergic transmission in the cortex.
Dopamine D1 through D5, all metabotropic Found throughout the forebrain, and involved in motor control, reward, and higher cognition.
Norepinephrine α1, α2, β1, β2, β3, all metabotropic In the body they run sympathetic fight-or-flight responses. In the brain they alert and arouse.
Serotonin 5-HT1 through 5-HT7 families, all metabotropic except 5-HT3 The subtypes differ in where they sit. 5-HT2 receptors are tied to mood, sleep, and cognition; 5-HT3 receptors to nausea.
Peptides Delta, kappa and mu opioid receptors, CCK, neurotensin, NPY and dozens more, all metabotropic Their jobs follow their location, and include the control of feeding, sexual behavior, and social behavior.

What counts as a neurotransmitter

A chemical is treated as a classical transmitter if the presynaptic neuron can make it and store it in the terminal, if it is released when action potentials arrive, if specific receptors on the postsynaptic membrane recognize it, if it changes the postsynaptic cell, and if blocking its release stops the presynaptic cell from having its effect.

The gas transmitters break several of those rules and are counted anyway. Each year the list of probable transmitters grows.

One neuron, more than one transmitter

A single presynaptic neuron can make and release more than one type of transmitter, which is called co-localization. Neuropeptides are often released this way, alongside a classical transmitter from the same terminal.

Which transmitter a given cell uses is the basis for naming it: a cell that runs on acetylcholine is cholinergic, one that runs on dopamine is dopaminergic, and so on through noradrenergic and serotonergic.

Where drugs get in

Nearly every psychoactive drug works by changing one of these systems at one step of the synaptic cycle. Those steps, and the drugs that act at each, are laid out on The Synapse.
