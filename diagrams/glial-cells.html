Glial Cells

Glia outnumber neurons in the human brain. Hundreds of types of neuron have been identified, but only four main kinds of glial cell: two that wrap axons in myelin, and two that keep the tissue supplied and clean.

Not just glue

Early neuroscientists took glia for filler holding the neurons in place, which is where the name comes from, since glia is Greek for glue. They do much more than that. Glia supply neurons with raw materials, chemical signals, and structural components, and they take part in information processing. The ratio is roughly one to one with neurons rather than the ten to one that older textbooks claimed.

Myelin, speed, and what happens when it goes

Whether it comes from an oligodendrocyte or a Schwann cell, myelin has the same effect: a large increase in the speed at which a signal travels down the axon, because the impulse jumps from one node of Ranvier to the next instead of creeping along the whole membrane. Where the insulation is compromised, as it is in multiple sclerosis, conduction slows or fails, and the symptoms follow whichever pathways lost their myelin.
