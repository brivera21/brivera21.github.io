    What you are looking at
    
    
    The first panel is the whole lab sequence drawn as one program. Each block is a lab you have already done, in the order the data go through them, and the bracket on the left is a for loop; every block inside it runs once per subject. The three flags at the top of the script switch groups of blocks on and off, so after fixing one subject you can re-run only the grand average instead of importing everything again. The MATLAB skeleton on the right has the real shape of the lab script, and it updates as you add subjects or channels to interpolate.
    
    
    The second panel builds the final figure of the project, the grand-average N400 at one electrode. The checklist beside it holds the plotting rules from Luck (2014): a reader has to be able to see the prestimulus baseline, find time zero and 0 µV, read the voltage scale and polarity, tell the conditions apart, and see the difference wave, all without leaving the figure.

    Why it matters for the pipeline
    
    
    A script is the record of an analysis. It runs the same way on every subject, it can be handed to someone else, and every parameter you chose in the earlier labs, the 0.1 Hz cutoff, the ±100 µV threshold, the 300–500 ms window, is written down where a reviewer can see it. The first draft comes from EEGLAB. Every menu action appends its command to EEG.history, so a script is mostly the history of one subject wrapped in a loop. The loop needs a predictable place to find each file, which is what the BIDS layout gives you (Gorgolewski et al., 2016; Pernet et al., 2019): sub-001/eeg/sub-001_task-N400_eeg.set, built with fullfile so the same script runs on Windows and macOS. A cell array of subject IDs drives the loop, and a matching cell array of channels to interpolate keeps the one thing that differs between subjects out of the code.
    
    
    The grand average, pop_gaverager, is the mean of the subjects' averaged ERPs. With two subjects it shows you whether the pipeline worked; with twenty it shows you the component, and it is what the statistics in the previous step are about. pop_ploterps draws it. Luck's rule for the drawing is that a figure without a prestimulus baseline gives the reader no way to tell signal from drift, and he labels such studies C.R.A.P., a Carelessly Reviewed Awful Publication. The checklist under the figure spells that rule out.
